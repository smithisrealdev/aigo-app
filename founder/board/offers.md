# Offers lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- Time to first result is excellent: a guest types one sentence and gets a day-by-day itinerary before signing up. Speed and effort, two of the four value drivers, are already good.
- The stack is real and already built: map, weather replan, budget, packing list, checklist, booking hand-off. Each solves a problem on the way to "a trip that goes well".
- Affiliate income rides on buyers who are already spending on flights and hotels, so the money is there.
- The backend is reused, so the test is cheaper than a fresh build.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It sells credits, not an outcome, in a commodity market.** A buyer does not want 50 credits; they want a trip planned. General AI chatbots produce an itinerary for free, and the brief names no reason a buyer would believe AiGo's is better. No proof, no guarantee, no named customer: "travellers worldwide in four groups" is not a market choice.
2. **The price ladder undercuts itself and the margin is unknown.** Travel Pass is 50 credits for $4.99; the 50-credit pack is $7.99, so the pack is dominated. The pass adds only 20 credits over free. A monthly, no-rollover pass does not fit people who plan a few trips a year. On top, AI cost per credit is unknown and the stores take 15 to 30 percent.
3. **No way to reach buyers.** Mobile acquisition is undecided, and web conversion, retention and revenue are unknown. An app with no channel adds cost, not customers.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one of the four groups has the worst pain and pays today? What does the web data say?
- How many web users have ever paid, and for what: pass or packs?
- What does one credit cost you in AI spend, by model?
- What does a generated trip earn in affiliate commission, on average?
- Why would a buyer believe this itinerary beats a free chatbot's? What proof exists?
- Could you charge per trip (say, one price for a finished, bookable plan) instead of per credit, and could that price double?
- What would you guarantee?
- Why mobile now, rather than fixing the offer on web first?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Produce the web numbers before any mobile build: sign-ups, paying users, revenue, repeat use, affiliate income. If there are effectively no payers, fix the offer on web first.
2. Establish AI cost per credit and show a positive margin on every paid tier after the store's 30 percent.
3. Pick one customer group and one market (the Thai-first proposal is a start) and rewrite the offer around their outcome, with a per-trip price tested against the credit model.
4. Fix the ladder so no tier is dominated, and remove the no-rollover penalty or justify it.
5. Name one acquisition channel and its measured cost per install, tested with a waitlist or small paid test.
