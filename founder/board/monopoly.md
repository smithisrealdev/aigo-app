# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The backend, the itinerary engine and the web product already exist, so mobile is a cheap bet on something built, not a bet from zero.
- "Thai-first" is the only hint of a small market that could be owned first. It is the most valuable line in the brief.
- The marketplace (clone a trip, creator earns a credit) is the seed of a network effect. Today it is a seed only.
- Two income lines (credits, affiliate), and affiliate does not pay the store commission.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No way to reach buyers.** Mobile acquisition is "not decided". The web's SEO pages and blog do not carry into a store app. An app with no distribution is not a business.
2. **Nothing a copycat cannot rebuild.** The user picks the AI model per message, so the intelligence is rented, not proprietary. The brief shows no technology ten times better, no scale advantage, no brand, and an unmeasured network effect. Any general AI assistant can also write a day-by-day itinerary; what AiGo does that they cannot is not stated.
3. **Four customer groups, worldwide, ten languages.** That is the opposite of a big share of a small market. On top of that, the margin on a credit is unknown and the stores take 15 to 30 percent of it.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Why mobile now? The last web commit was March 2026 and the brief gives no reason.
- What do you know about Thai travellers (or travellers to Thailand: which is it?) that other planners do not?
- How do the first 100 mobile users arrive, and what does each cost?
- How many web users, payers and repeat planners exist today? If the web has not found demand, why would the app?
- How many public trips exist, and how many have been cloned by a stranger?
- What does one credit cost you in AI spend?
- Who else sells an AI trip planner to the same buyer, and what stops them matching you in month six?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one small market and say it in a sentence (for example one traveller group on one route or destination). Cut the other three groups from v1 messaging.
2. Show the web numbers: users, payers, retention, affiliate income. If there are none, validate on the web first; do not build mobile.
3. Name one channel for the first 100 mobile users, with a tested cost per install.
4. Show the AI cost per credit, proving a positive margin after the store commission.
5. Name the one durable advantage to build (most plausibly the trip marketplace for the chosen market) and make v1 serve it.

If conditions 2 and 3 cannot be met, this is a PASS.
