# Product lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The core moment is real and already built: type a wish, watch a day-by-day trip appear. Guests reach it before signing up.
- A phone is where a trip is actually lived (map, today's plan, weather replan). Mobile could be the better product, not a port.
- The backend exists, so the money can go into craft instead of plumbing.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No one thing.** Four customer groups, four tabs, nine flows, a marketplace, a referral scheme and a credit economy. This is the web app shrunk, not a product designed for a phone. Nothing in it is done brilliantly because everything is in it.
2. **The meter is the experience.** Users pick an AI model per message at 1 to 15 credits, and a trip costs 10 plus 2 per extra day. The customer is asked to do pricing arithmetic before they get delight. Free covers about one trip, so the second session opens on a paywall.
3. **It fails where a travel app is used.** No offline mode, booking hands off to partner sites, shared trips do not sync live, and the look is borrowed from a cooking app. Each is a seam, and the trip itself (abroad, poor signal, with friends) sits on all of them.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is AiGo Mobile, and for which one traveller?
- Why mobile now? What does the phone do that theaigo.co cannot?
- What do web users do after the first itinerary: come back, book, or leave? (Retention and conversion are unknown.)
- What does a credit cost you? Without it, nobody knows if the free trip is affordable.
- What happens when the user opens the app in a foreign city with no signal?
- Why does a travel product wear another product's design system?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pick one traveller** (the Thai-first choice implies Thai leisure travellers going abroad; say so) and write the one-sentence pitch.
2. **Cut v1 to one job: plan a trip, then carry it.** Cut Explore and the marketplace, cloning-for-credits, the model picker (pick the model for them), manual expense budgeting, price alerts, invite roles, and credit packs (one free allowance, one pass). Two tabs at most: Plan and Trips. Keep booking links only inside a place card.
3. **Add the one thing cut from scope: a saved itinerary readable offline.** Without it the app is worse than a screenshot.
4. **Script the first five minutes and build to it:** open, no login, no tabs to learn, one prompt box with a Thai example. Type one line. The itinerary streams in within seconds, day one on a map. Move a stop with a thumb; times recalculate. Save it; only then ask for an account. No credit count shown until the second trip.
5. **Show web retention numbers first.** If people do not return to the web version, a native shell will not fix it.
