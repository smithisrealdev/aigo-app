# Board summary: AiGo Mobile

**Vote: 3 fund if, 0 fund, 0 pass. Average score 3.7 / 10** (Offers 4, Monopoly 3, Product 4).

The Monopoly lens adds: if conditions 1 and 3 below cannot be met, its vote becomes PASS.

The three lenses are summaries of published frameworks. No member speaks for the authors of those frameworks.

## Risks more than one member raised

1. **No way to reach buyers** (Offers, Monopoly). Mobile acquisition is undecided, and the web's SEO pages do not carry into a store app.
2. **Web demand is unproven** (all three). Users, payers, retention and affiliate income are unknown. If the web has not found demand, a native app will not create it.
3. **No single customer** (all three). Four traveller groups, worldwide, ten languages.
4. **Margin on a credit is unknown** (all three). AI cost per credit is not known, and the stores take 15 to 30 percent of in-app purchases.
5. **Nothing a general AI chatbot cannot also do is stated** (Offers, Monopoly). The intelligence is rented; there is no named proof, guarantee or advantage.
6. **The credit meter is the experience** (Offers, Product). Users choose a model at 1 to 15 credits a message; free covers about one trip; the 50-credit pack ($7.99) is dominated by the pass ($4.99 for 50).

## Raised by one member

- **Product:** v1 is the web app shrunk, with no one thing; it has no offline mode, where a travel app is actually used; the look is borrowed from a cooking app.
- **Monopoly:** the marketplace is the only seed of a network effect, and it is unmeasured.

## Where the members disagree

- **The marketplace.** Product: cut Explore and cloning from v1. Monopoly: the marketplace is the most plausible durable advantage, so v1 should serve it. Not resolved. The web's clone counts would decide it.
- **How to charge.** Offers: test a per-trip price against credits. Product: keep one free allowance and one pass, hide the credit count until the second trip, drop packs and the model picker.

## Conditions, merged

Evidence to gather before building:

- [ ] 1. Web numbers: sign-ups, paying users, revenue, repeat use, affiliate income, and how many public trips a stranger has cloned. If there are effectively no payers or returners, fix the offer on web first. (founder's own data)
- [ ] 2. AI cost per credit by model, showing a positive margin on every paid tier after the store's commission. (`/founder-cfo`)
- [ ] 3. One acquisition channel for the first 100 mobile users, with a tested cost per install. (`/founder-marketing`, `/founder-launch`)
- [ ] 4. Why a traveller would believe this beats a free chatbot's itinerary. (`/founder-competitors`, `/founder-consumer`)

Decisions that shape the design:

- [ ] 5. One traveller and one market, in one sentence. The Thai-first proposal implies Thai leisure travellers going abroad; confirm or correct.
- [ ] 6. Cut v1 to one job: plan a trip, then carry it. Two tabs at most.
- [ ] 7. Add a saved itinerary readable offline.
- [ ] 8. Remove the model picker; choose the model for the user.
- [ ] 9. Fix the price ladder so no tier is dominated; test a per-trip price. (`/founder-pricing`)
- [ ] 10. Script the first five minutes: no login, one prompt box with a Thai example, the itinerary streams in, move a stop with a thumb, save, and only then ask for an account.
- [ ] 11. Decide the marketplace: in v1 as the advantage to build, or out.

## The strongest version the board can see

This differs from what was pitched. Not "the AiGo web app on a phone for four kinds of traveller", but a trip companion for one traveller, most plausibly Thai leisure travellers going abroad: open the app, type one line in Thai, watch a day-by-day plan appear on a map, adjust it with a thumb, and carry it through the trip with it readable offline. No model picker and no credit arithmetic before the first plan; one free allowance and one simple paid step; booking links only inside a place card, where affiliate income pays no store commission. Whether public trips for that one market become the thing a copycat cannot rebuild depends on numbers the board does not have.
